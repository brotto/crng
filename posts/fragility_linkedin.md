# LinkedIn Post — CRNG-Fragility Monitor

Global markets are flashing a signal we've never seen before. Not in 2008. Not in COVID. Not ever.

--

I built a statistical fragility monitor that tracks rolling kurtosis across key commodities — a measure of how "fat" the tails of price distributions are getting. Fat tails mean one thing: the system is loading energy for a Seneca Cliff event, where decline is sudden and nonlinear.

The current readings are historically unprecedented.

Average kurtosis across key commodities: K = +9.05 — roughly 2x the levels observed during COVID.

NATGAS: K = +16.47 (extreme energy loading)
Gold: K = +7.39 at $4,694/oz (record price, record fragility)

Traditional volatility measures (VIX, standard deviation) miss this entirely. They assume Gaussian distributions. Kurtosis captures what Gaussians cannot: the probability of events that "shouldn't happen."

--

Steve Keen's recent analysis of Strait of Hormuz vulnerability was the catalyst. 20% of global oil flows through a single chokepoint. But vulnerability is not fragility. Fragility is what the data already shows: price distributions deforming in ways that precede rupture.

The tool doesn't predict WHEN. It measures HOW LOADED the system is right now. Think of it as a seismograph for markets — it won't tell you the day of the earthquake, but it will tell you the tectonic stress is at historic levels.

--

The connection to CRNG theory is direct: we detect patterns through contingency rather than modeling mechanics. We don't need to know WHY oil prices will spike. We observe that the statistical geometry of price movements has already changed.
