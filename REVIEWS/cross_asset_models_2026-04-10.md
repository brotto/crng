## What the table says

### 1. Two families of assets

- **Crypto (BTC, ETH)** — CRNG roughly matches the shape on both train and
  validation: the train-time kurtosis is in the right neighborhood and the
  OOS kurtosis is within ~20% of the real value. This is the family for which
  the current cascade model does its best work.
- **Tradfi (Gold, SP500, Oil, EURUSD, USDJPY)** — CRNG consistently undershoots
  the real kurtosis, especially out-of-sample. Gold and SP500 are the extreme
  cases: their validation windows contain a regime much more tail-heavy than
  their training windows (K_real jumps from 4.8 → 14–18), and the CRNG
  calibrated on the calmer train regime has no way of knowing this would
  happen. This is the honest face of "descriptive, not predictive": the model
  reproduces what it saw, not what it wasn't shown.

### 1b. NEW FINDING: `from_data` has a silent degeneracy mode

Running the calibration across all seven assets exposed a failure mode of
`crng.from_data` I had not documented before:

| Asset  | Train K_real | Calibrated `target_kurtosis` | Calibrated `amplification` | Verdict     |
|--------|-------------:|------------------------------:|----------------------------:|-------------|
| BTC    | 6.515        | 5.177                         | 3.059                       | OK          |
| ETH    | 9.005        | 5.375                         | 3.125                       | OK          |
| Oil    | 5.739        | 4.988                         | 2.994                       | OK          |
| USDJPY | 7.116        | 4.469                         | 2.759                       | OK (low)    |
| EURUSD | 4.080        | 3.513                         | 1.425                       | OK (low)    |
| **Gold**  | **4.761** | **3.000**                     | **0.000**                   | **DEGENERATE** |
| **SP500** | **4.805** | **3.000**                     | **0.000**                   | **DEGENERATE** |

For Gold and SP500, the iterative calibration in `from_data` collapsed to a
near-Gaussian state: `target_kurtosis=3, amplification=0`. The resulting CRNG
instance is effectively equivalent to `iid_gaussian` for shape purposes.

**Why it happens:** `from_data` iterates across 5 rounds, clamping the
amplification update by `3 × measured_vol_clustering`. When both the real
kurtosis is modest (K < ~5) and the vol-clustering is small, the clamp drives
the amplification toward zero, and on the next iteration the kurtosis
estimate also falls toward 3, reinforcing the collapse.

**Both affected models explicitly document this** via a
`honest_misses.calibration_degeneracy` block that names the failure mode and
tells downstream users to treat the model as equivalent to `iid_gaussian`.
This is SPECS.md P4 in action: the gap is measured and reported, not hidden.

**What this means for the package:** `from_data` is a convenience path, not a
guaranteed calibration. For assets whose training-window kurtosis is close to
Gaussian, the iterative calibration can silently degenerate. The honest fix
(for a future version) is to raise instead of returning a degenerate instance
— but until then, any user of `from_data` should cross-check the returned
`target_kurtosis` and `amplification` and compare against the real training
kurtosis. The `models/*_v1.yaml` files do this by construction.

### 2. The vol-clustering gap is universal

Across every asset the validation real `vol_acf_lag1` is positive (0.07–0.24),
while the CRNG validation `vol_acf_lag1` is essentially zero or slightly
negative for 6 of 7 (USDJPY is the only one where CRNG produces a comparable
positive value, 0.080 vs 0.066). This confirms a structural claim we've
flagged in the README and in every `honest_misses` block:

> **The current cascade model produces fat tails but does not produce
> persistent volatility. This is its main architectural weakness.**

Reproducing fat tails requires the cascade to fire in bursts; reproducing
volatility clustering requires those bursts to autocorrelate. The present
calibration pipeline does the first but not the second.

### 3. Train-vs-val regime change is itself the story

The huge gaps between real train K and real val K for Gold (+198%), SP500
(+280%) and Oil (+106%) are not CRNG's fault — they are the market changing
regime between the two halves of our 5-year snapshot. For a descriptive-
generative model this is diagnostically valuable: it tells us which assets
had a regime break in the snapshot window (Gold, SP500, Oil) and which did
not (BTC, ETH, EURUSD, USDJPY).

A predictive model would have to handle this. A descriptive-generative model
can only say "I was shown regime A; here is a shape generator for regime A."
The honest thing to do — and what each `honest_misses` block does — is to
report the gap and attribute it to regime change, not to hide it.

### 4. Amplitude is a different axis entirely

All seven models have a `std` ratio in the 10x–25x range between CRNG output
and real returns. This is by design: CRNG generates a dimensionless shape
that must be rescaled before it can be interpreted as returns, prices, or
VaR. Each model's `honest_misses.std_train` and `std_val` blocks document this.
Downstream users should never treat CRNG output as calibrated in units of
log-returns without explicit rescaling.

## What these models can and cannot be used for

**Can:**
- Replace `np.random.normal()` in Monte Carlo simulations where the goal is
  shape realism (fat tails + skew) rather than amplitude fidelity.
- Stress-test strategies by generating synthetic series whose tail frequency
  is roughly comparable to the training asset.
- Benchmark: compare against `iid_gaussian` to see how much shape a generator
  is adding on top of pure Gaussian noise.

**Cannot:**
- Forecast prices, returns, volatility, regime, or any time-indexed quantity.
- Be used for risk calculations (VaR, expected shortfall) at face value — the
  amplitude mismatch means a naive pipeline will massively under- or
  overstate risk depending on direction.
- Replace a proper volatility model where persistence matters (GARCH etc.),
  because the cascade does not reproduce vol clustering.

## What changes relative to the legacy preset claims

The legacy presets (`gold()`, `btc()`, `eth()`, etc.) in `crng/presets.py`
were hardcoded with fixed target kurtosis values (e.g. btc → K=219) that came
from a different, buggy measurement regime (`stats()` calling `np.diff()`
internally and benchmarks running against unfrozen live data).

These formal models replace that with:
- a frozen snapshot (immutable, SHA-verified),
- disjoint train/val windows,
- the `from_data` calibration path used as the actual instantiation route,
- target vs achieved fingerprints reported side-by-side,
- honest_misses blocks for every gap.

The legacy presets remain in the code for backward compatibility but the
README no longer uses them in the headline tables. The models in `models/*.yaml`
are now the canonical way to report CRNG's real performance on real data.
